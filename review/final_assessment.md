## 2. Most consequential remaining objections

1. **(b) Generality: one category, one detector, synthetic composites.** All main results use 138 composited pushpin photos and SALAD (abstract; l. 446-455). The paper's own conclusion asks that "logical anomaly detectors should be evaluated by recall together with inspection cost" (l. 444-445). That broad claim needs at least a second location-bound category (e.g. splicing connectors or screw bag with a placement rule) and a second detector (e.g. a CSAD- or GCAD-style method) under the same protocol.
2. **(b) Train-test match of the remedy.** The footprint heads are fine-tuned on "relocation, sham or vacancy" edits from the same rule (l. 247-249; supp. l. 99-103). They are then tested on the same edit family. The paper admits "the gain is established for that setting" (l. 450-451). The paper reproduces the published AUROC on the 91 native logical pushpin faults (l. 357-359), but it does not report inspection cost or localization for the fine-tuned heads on them, nor any effect on structural or other LOCO categories. Without that, the remedy may be overfitting to the edit generator.
3. **(b) Control validity and seed dependence.** The pre-specified primary contrast is against class-wide heads that fail the competence check (l. 331-333; Fig. 6 dagger). The only clean mechanistic evidence is one continuation seed (Sec. 4.3; 11.50 to 7.07). Two seeds disagree on sham alarms and top-2 (l. 322-329). The paper needs 3 or more seeds and a competent class-wide control, or else a softer causal claim than "This cost is largely determined by the training target" (l. 435-436).
4. **(a) Scope dilution by Secs. 5-6.** The juice branch analysis and especially the GlaS/HVSMR masked-GNN degree pathway (Sec. 6, Fig. 8) are only loosely tied to "budgeted inspection". They take about a page that the core claims need, for example native-fault results and a limitations paragraph. The degree-pathway result is a separate finding presented in compressed form. Shortening it to a brief transfer note, or moving it to the supplement, would sharpen the paper.
5. **(a) Metric framing versus sPRO/top-k.** The claim that image AUROC, top-k and region overlap "do not reveal" the cost (l. 433-434) is partly undercut by the paper's own Area column. Area rises from 81.6 to 96.3/89.6, and the class-wide heads score 39.3. The text concedes the direction agrees (l. 319-321). The paper should state more carefully what R/C adds: operating-point calibration and scene gating rather than ranking. It should also explain why the released model's high top-3 rate (94.2%) coexists with C=11.07, namely saturation at a low t_c.

## 3. Presentation defects

- **Cutoff inequality inconsistency.** Eq. (2) and Alg. 1 l. 6 queue compartments with g >= t_c. The fixed-cost cutoff is "the 35th largest untouched score" that "admits at most 0.5 untouched compartments" (l. 183-186). With >= this queues 35/69 = 0.507 > 0.5. Only the supplement says this cutoff uses "a strict comparison" (supp. l. 21-24). Main-text Eq. (2) should state it.
- **Fig. 5d encoding overload.** In the legend, filled squares stand for the colours (released, class-wide, footprint). Squares also mean "fixed-cost cutoff". The two class-wide fixed-cost points (0.65/39.5 and 0.64/39.1, supp. Tab. 4) overlap, so seed 1 is invisible. The text never reports the class-wide fixed-cost recall (~39%) that the panel shows.
- **Fig. 1a.** The valid-sham panel draws a curved, rotation-like arrow and a re-oriented pin. The method translates "without resampling" (l. 128). The heading says "one held-out photo", but the panel is BioRender art, not a photo.
- **Fig. 4.** The panel label "(a)" collides with the "100%" tick. The caption says "more than the cutoff", while the text uses "at least 0.5" (l. 283). The caption does not say the curves are for the released model.
- **Fig. 6.** Row labels and axis annotations ("useful: <= -1", "floor: -5", "guard: +5") are about 5 pt at print size. The pink "Upstream-only" rows are hard to tell apart from the purple rows.
- **Fig. 8.** Annotation text ("50 by construction", "+15.3") is tiny. The red highlight introduces a new colour meaning (red = masked GNN) that is not used elsewhere. The caption's "78 GlaS fields, 12 donors" is not stated in the Sec. 6 text.
- **Bold lead-ins.** The intro uses bold run-in contribution labels: **evaluation design**, **diagnosis**, **remedy**, **transfer checks** (l. 63-73). These read as an inline bullet list.
- **Undefined or late-defined terms.** "competence criterion" is used in l. 333 and Fig. 6 ("competence check") before or without an explicit name in Sec. 3.5. "Region area" is normalised by 0.05 in the text but reported in percent in Tab. 1. "Fusion" appears in Sec. 5 without a formula. The meaning of "d_u - a_e" (l. 413) relies on a_e, defined only two lines earlier as a binary state.
- **Numbers.** Abstract, Fig. 1, Tab. 1, text and supp. Tab. 3 agree (11.07; 0.20/0.45; 10.88, 10.62, 12.80, 12.55; -7.2/-1.4; +84.3/+79.8; 87.5/33.7/46.6). No numeric conflicts were found apart from the >= issue above.
- **Minor.** Tab. 1 2nd = 15.00 for the class-wide heads with ties "broken by compartment index" (l. 193). A brief note would help explain why the second target is always ranked last. The supplement reports "development AUROC 97.3%", which coincides with the U-Net IoU of 97.3% (l. 154). These are distinct numbers, but the coincidence invites confusion.
